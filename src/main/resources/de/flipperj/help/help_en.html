FlipperJ – User guide

FlipperJ turns any picture into an FLI, AFLI or IFLI picture for the Commodore 64 and writes it as a runnable C64 program (.prg) and/or as raw data. Everything you change is shown at once in the preview.

Quick start

  1. Open a picture: File ▸ Open picture…, the Select… button, or drag a file onto the window.
  2. If the picture is not 320×200 (or 160×200), the Crop / scale dialog opens. Choose the part you want and press OK.
  3. Choose the mode (FLI, AFLI or IFLI). With Live preview switched on, the result appears on the right after every change.
  4. Try the dithering and the prepare sliders until you like the result.
  5. Press Convert. FlipperJ writes the .prg (and the raw data, if switched on) to the output file shown at the top.
  6. On the C64 or in VICE: LOAD"NAME",8 and RUN (or SYS 2061). The picture stays on screen until you reset the C64.

FLI, AFLI and IFLI

ModeResolutionColors
FLI (multicolor)160×200, double-wide pixelsPer 4×8 cell: one color from the color RAM, plus two colors per cell and pixel line, plus one background color for the whole picture.
AFLI (hires FLI)320×200Per 8×1 cell (one cell, one pixel line): two colors.
IFLI (multicolor interlace)"virtually" 320×200Two FLI pictures shown alternately every frame, the second one shifted by one pixel. The eye mixes them into more colors and finer detail. On a real monitor it flickers slightly.

The FLI bug: on a real C64 the leftmost three character columns (24 pixels) of every FLI line cannot show the picture's colors. FlipperJ knows this and only uses the background color there. With Render FLI bug you see these columns as the C64 shows them.

Entries marked "planned" in the mode list are not available yet.

Opening pictures

FlipperJ reads every common PC format (PNG, JPEG, GIF, BMP, WebP, TIFF, PSD, TGA, PCX, …) and C64 pictures: Koala, Art Studio, Advanced Art Studio, Drazpaint, Drazlace, FLI, FLI Designer, FLI Graph, FLI Editor, AFLI, Gunpaint, Funpaint and FlipperJ's own raw files. C64 pictures are drawn with the selected source palette; interlace pictures are mixed as the eye sees them.

The open dialog shows "All image formats" by default; the list also has a filter for C64 pictures. When you open a new picture, the output file name follows it (same name, .prg).

Crop & scale

Pictures that are not C64-sized open this dialog. You can open it again any time with Crop / scale… (bottom) or File ▸ Crop / scale….

  • Frame: drag inside the frame to move it, drag the corner handle to resize it, drag outside to draw a new frame. The fields X, Y, Width and Height set it exactly. Whole image and Max. centered frame are shortcuts.
  • Display aspect: the shape of the final picture. C64 PAL (about 1.50:1) is right for a PAL monitor, Square pixels (1.60:1) keeps the picture exactly as on the PC, NTSC (about 1.20:1), Free = no rule. Lock crop to target aspect keeps the frame in that shape.
  • Scale mode: Stretch fills 320×200 and may distort, Fit (letterbox) keeps the whole frame and adds borders, Fill (crop) fills the screen and cuts off what sticks out, 1:1 takes exactly 320×200 pixels without scaling.
  • Filter: how pixels are recalculated. Area average (smooth) is smooth and best for photos, Nearest neighbour keeps hard pixel edges (pixel art).
  • Border color…: color of the borders added by Fit.

Reframe in the preview: right-click the left preview (or File ▸ Reframe in preview) to choose another part of the original directly there: mouse wheel = size, drag with the left button = move, right-click or Enter = apply, Esc = cancel. The whole original is kept, so you can reframe as often as you like.

Source palette & matching

The source palette tells FlipperJ which RGB colors the 16 C64 colors have when it compares them with your picture. Pepto and Colodore are measured from real C64s; others (DeeKay, PALette, Community Colors, …) are popular alternatives. Custom… opens the palette editor: change single colors, load .vpl, .gpl or hex files, save as .vpl.

Match in sets how "similar" is measured:

  • YUV (default): separates brightness from color, like a TV. Weight U and Weight V decide how much the color differences count compared with brightness (50 = normal). Lower values = brightness matters more.
  • RGB: RGB distance weighted by how bright each channel looks.
  • HSL: hue, saturation and lightness.

Respect luma ramp: additionally punishes colors that are too bright or too dark. Helps to keep gradients and faces in the right order of brightness.

Prepare

These sliders change the picture before the conversion (see the Pre-convert view): Contrast and Brightness (-100 to 100), Gamma % (below 100 = brighter middle tones, above 100 = darker) and Saturation % (0 = grey, above 100 = more colorful). The C64 has few dark and few saturated colors, so a little more contrast and saturation often helps.

Dithering & color selection

Dithering mixes C64 colors in fine patterns so that the eye sees the colors in between.

  • Dither: None = flat areas. Floyd-Steinberg, Atkinson, Jarvis-Judice-Ninke, Stucki, Sierra Lite spread the error to neighbouring pixels (natural, grainy look; Atkinson is lighter and more contrasty). Bayer 2×2/4×4/8×8 use a fixed pattern (regular, "retro" look, calmer in animations).
  • Strength %: how much error is spread. Lower = calmer, higher = more detail and more noise.
  • Bleeding: on = the dither error may run into the neighbouring cells (smoother transitions); off = every cell is dithered on its own (cleaner cell edges).
  • Serpentine: scans every second line from right to left. Avoids diagonal "worm" patterns.
  • Passes: after the first run, FlipperJ chooses the colors of each cell again from what the dithering "wanted" and keeps the best run. More passes = better, but slower.
  • Background: the one color all cells share. Auto picks the best one; or choose a fixed color.

Every group has a Defaults button. Sliders: the small arrows move by 1 (hold to repeat), the mark shows the default value and the knob snaps to it, a double click resets the slider.

Destination palette

The destination palette is only used to display the result – choose the one that looks most like your monitor or emulator. It does not change the C64 data.

  • Render FLI bug: shows the three left columns as the C64 does.
  • PAL pixel aspect in previews: shows the pictures slightly wider, as on a PAL monitor.

Previews, zoom and pan

Left: Original or Pre-convert (after cropping and preparing). Right: Result or Error map (bright = big difference to the pre-converted picture, dark = good match).

  • Mouse wheel = zoom, drag with the right mouse button = pan, double click = fit. The buttons (−, +, Fit, 4×) do the same. 4× shows 4 screen pixels per C64 pixel.
  • Link zooms and pans both previews together. Grid shows pixel and 8×8 cell lines when zoomed in.
  • Live preview converts automatically after every change. Switch it off for slow computers and press Convert instead.
  • Save pre-convert and Save result & error map write PNG files, e.g. to compare or to show them.
  • Cancel stops a running conversion.

Output: .prg and raw data

  • Write runnable .prg: picture plus displayer in one file. Start with RUN or SYS 2061.
  • Write raw data: for your own demo or display code. Per frame (IFLI: suffix _a and _b): NAME_bitmap.bin (8000 bytes), NAME_screens.bin (8×1024 bytes, picture line y uses screen y AND 7), NAME_colorram.bin (1000 bytes, multicolor) and NAME_bg.bin (1 byte), plus one combined file NAME.fli / .afl / .ifl with load address $3C00 (color RAM $3C00, background $3FF0/$3FF1, screens $4000, bitmap $6000, IFLI frame B from $8000). FlipperJ and MuleJ can open these combined files again.
Memory map of the .prg
$0801BASIC line SYS 2061
$0900–$0BFFtables and displayer code
$3C00–$3FE7color RAM data (copied to $D800)
$4000–$5FFF8 screens
$6000–$7F3Fbitmap
$8000–$BF3FIFLI only: frame B, copied to $C000 at start

Lines 197–199 of an FLI picture cannot get their own colors (no bad lines are possible there); they repeat the colors of line 196. FlipperJ takes this into account.

Settings and language

File ▸ Settings… (on macOS: FlipperJ ▸ Settings…): language, live preview at start, grid, linked previews, PAL aspect, which outputs to write, and the output folder for new pictures (next to the input picture, or the last folder you chose). Restore defaults resets everything.

Language: menu Language or the settings. "System language" follows the language of your computer. FlipperJ speaks English, German, Polish, Swedish and Dutch.

FLI test picture for VICE

Help ▸ FLI test picture for VICE… writes a test program and a PNG that shows how the screen must look. Load it in VICE (PAL) or on a real C64 and compare:

  • Left 3 columns (FLI bug): line 0 black, all other lines light grey.
  • Color bar: every line its own color, repeating every 15 lines without gaps or doubled lines.
  • Barcode: the line number in binary, white = 1, dark grey = 0.
  • Right area: line 0 white, the first line of every character row red, line 196 yellow, the rest blue. Lines 197–199 repeat line 196.

A line with the colors of its neighbour points to a timing problem of the displayer – please report it.

Command line

With arguments FlipperJ runs without a window:

flipperj [options] picture.png output.prg
flipperj --test-picture fli-test.prg

Important options: --mode fli|afli|ifli, --palette NAME|FILE, --space rgb|yuv|hsl, --dither NAME (none, fs, atkinson, jarvis, stucki, sierra, bayer2, bayer4, bayer8), --strength N, --no-bleeding, --serpentine, --passes N, --bg N, --brightness/--contrast/--gamma/--saturation N, --scale stretch|fit|fill, --raw, --no-prg, --preview FILE.png. flipperj --help lists all of them.

Tips for good results

  • Choose a picture with a clear subject and not too many small details; faces and big shapes work best.
  • Try IFLI for photos with soft gradients, AFLI for line art and hires detail.
  • If the result looks muddy: raise contrast, lower the U/V weights or switch on Respect luma ramp.
  • If it looks noisy: lower the dither strength or try Bayer 4×4.
  • Use the Error map to find areas that need help, and the 4× zoom with grid to check cells.
